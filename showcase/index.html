Browse equirectangular 360° panoramas and VR skyboxes shared publicly by photographers, 3D artists and game developers. Every example opens in the free browser-based 360° panorama viewer.

What gets listed here

The public gallery is opt-in and new, so it grows one share at a time. These are the kinds of scenes creators publish most often:

  • Nature and landscapes: lakes, coastlines, dunes and forest light in 2:1 equirectangular projection.
  • Sci-fi and space: nebulae, orbital scenes and neon skylines generated for game or film look-dev.
  • Architecture and virtual tours: interiors, showrooms and real-estate walkthroughs.
  • Fantasy and stylized skyboxes: temple ruins, sunset skies and stylized environments for game engines.

How to Share and Publish Your Panorama

  1. Create or Upload Your Panorama: Generate a scene in our AI Studio or open any 2:1 equirectangular photo from your device.
  2. Open Share Dialog: Click the Share button in the navigation bar to generate an anonymous, secure view link.
  3. Opt-in to Public Showcase: Tick the "List in public community showcase" checkbox. Shares remain completely private by default until you opt in.
  4. Instant Interactive WebGL Link: Anyone with your link can rotate the scene, switch to Little Planet mode, or inspect in mobile VR cardboard. You can unlist or delete your share link at any time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a 360 panorama showcase?

A showcase is a curated public collection of real 2:1 equirectangular panoramas, AI-generated skyboxes, and HDRI environment maps published by community members to study spatial composition, lighting, and seamless boundary wrapping.

Are the panoramas in the showcase free to view?

Yes. Every showcase entry opens in our free online 360 Panorama Viewer with smooth damped inertia panning, Little Planet projection, and VR cardboard mode without needing an account or app installation.

What resolution and aspect ratio are supported?

Panoramas should use a standard 2:1 width-to-height equirectangular aspect ratio (such as 2048×1024, 4096×2048, or 8192×4096) in PNG or JPEG format.

Studying spatial composition in 360° environments

Public examples are useful for 3D level designers, architectural photographers and digital artists, especially when you can rotate the full sphere instead of judging a flat preview:

  • Horizon placement and eyeline balance: see where the horizon sits and whether it feels stable in a headset.
  • Zenith and Nadir Management: Study how artists blend straight-up celestial textures and straight-down ground surfaces to avoid stretching artifacts.
  • Seam Quality Inspection: Rotate 360 degrees to inspect where the left and right borders join, observing how multi-band gradient healing eliminates visible cuts.
  • Light Direction and Specular Bounces: Analyze primary illumination angles, key light contrast, and ambient fill in high dynamic range environments.
