What Is a Map to Globe Converter?

A map to globe converter takes a flat, rectangular image and wraps it around a sphere so you can rotate the result like a planet. It is also called a globe maker, an image to globe tool, or a sphere projection tool; the operation is always the same: read every pixel of the image, convert its X and Y coordinates into longitude and latitude, and sample the pixel that lies in that direction.

Because the maths is pure geometry, the result is instant and lossless. What matters for quality is the source image: an equirectangular world map wraps without artefacts, while a Mercator wall map squeezes the poles the moment it becomes spherical. Any equirectangular 360 photo is already in the right projection, so loading one here produces the classic tiny planet view.

Which Image Format Wraps Best on a Sphere?

  • Aspect ratio: 2:1 is ideal, for example 4096×2048. A square image wraps once around the equator and pinches at both poles.
  • Longitude origin: the centre column becomes longitude 0°, and the left and right edges meet at 180°.
  • File types: PNG, JPEG, WEBP and GIF all work; use PNG or high-quality JPEG at roughly 4096 px wide for crisp continents.
  • Flat maps: Mercator and Robinson maps still wrap, but pre-project to equirectangular for a believable planet.
